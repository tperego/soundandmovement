One of the first problems in building the work was designing a choreography that could be played and experienced through randomly selected snapshots.

Noora characterised the pollen through movement ideas developed in rehearsal with dancers, tracing their paths and behaviours. With Eduardo she then translated these into trajectories in Maya, starting from each flower and fitting the shape, size, angle and movement of its petals and stamens.

This led to three distinct types of pollen per flower. To add variety, each trajectory is rotated around the flower's centre: the same path repeated in 10 instances over 360° of azimuth, which produced the origami-like patterns in [the poppy post](/blog/2024/08/randomness-colour-and-the-poppy/).

When the bee hits a flower, 1–10 pollen are released from types A, B or C, at any of the 10 angles on the horizontal plane. The combinations in space are unpredictable, which gives great variety but raises questions about the choreographic relationship between paths:

- What of the movement is understandable?
- How much of the characterisation survives?
- Were the individual paths ever meant to play together, or only individually?

I will follow this up with Noora at the end of the work.
