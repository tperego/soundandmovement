Tommaso Perego · Senior Lecturer in Sound and Music, University of East London

How sound moves, and how we feel it move.

I compose and research sonic movement: the sense of motion we perceive through spatial audio. My work joins wave field synthesis and ambisonics with dance, theatre, visuals and interactive media, and asks how sight, body and sound shape what we hear.

Current research

New · Organised Sound, 2026

Sonic Movement: the perception of movement through sound as a case of multimodality integration in Spatial Audio Composition

The article sets movement created by sound beside movement created by dancers' bodies, and works through three case studies that mix sound, visuals, staging and performance. It argues that the feeling of sonic movement depends on intermediality and multimodal perception, especially in spatial audio compositions built around movement gestures, and draws out what this means for staging the listener's experience and participation.

About

Tommaso Perego is a composer, sound designer and researcher. He is Senior Lecturer in Sound and Music at the University of East London.

His PhD at Goldsmiths, University of London (Unit of Sound Practice Research), Sonic Choreosophia, explored spatial audio in multimodal contexts, applying wave field synthesis and ambisonics to dance choreography and theatre in collaboration with choreographers, theatre makers and visual artists.

Before that he trained at the Conservatorio di Musica Giuseppe Verdi in Milan, with diplomas in composition, double bass, music technology and orchestral conducting.
