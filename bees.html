Song of the Bees

A practice-based research project exploring movement expressed through sound and visuals in an immersive, interactive context.

With Noora (choreography), Eduardo (3D trajectories, Maya) and Kush (Unity development).

Project outline

The project looks at where the semantics of music, sound, space and movement meet, and how that interplay changes as the audience moves from passive listener to engaged, interactive participant.

It builds on earlier steps in which multimodality was tested by adding visual feedback through dancers (Stranded, 2014) and through guided listener movement and proprioception (I Hear You See Me, 2016), changing listening angles and perspectives on the movement of sound.

Interactivity adds new challenges. In a game the listener is no longer only a participant but "the protagonist, the decision maker" (Burgun 2015). Decision-making, exploration and self-progression become part of the listening experience, and the choreography of sound and visuals needs generative and procedural approaches.

Research questions

  1. How can a choreographic narrative be built through sound so that it is perceived meaningfully from the ever-changing perspective of the player?
  2. Does a sonic choreography suffer or benefit from the dynamic change of perspective caused by the player's movements? Could interaction distract from focused listening?
  3. How does this context relate to the proscenium or ritual understanding of a movement (dance) performance?
  4. How does this adaptive listening relate to other modes of listening (reduced, deep, etc.)?
  5. Can this framework of listening in new media offer insights to current audio technologies and to cross-disciplinary sound practice?
